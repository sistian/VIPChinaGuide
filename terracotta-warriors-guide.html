Why Visit the Terracotta Army with a Guide?

The Terracotta Army is not a museum — it is an excavation. Emperor Qin Shi Huang buried an estimated 8,000 life-sized clay soldiers, 130 chariots, and 150 c avalry horses to guard his tomb in the afterlife. Two millennia later, farmers digging a well uncovered one of the most astonishing archaeological finds in history. Standing in Pit 1 surrounded by these silent warriors is one of the most moving experiences in Chinese travel.

2,200 years old. Still standing. Still magnificent.

What You Will See

Pit 1 — Army Hall

The largest pit, housing 6,000 soldiers in battle formation. The most visually stunning of the three pits. Your guide reveals the military strategy encoded in the formation — and why the army faces east.

Pit 2 — Command Center

The smallest but most strategically important pit, containing cavalry, infantry, and archers. Features the only kneeling archer figure on public display.

Pit 3 — Commanders' Pit

The exclusive officers' section — only 68 warriors and 6 war chariots. Believed to be the command headquarters of the entire army.

Terracotta Warriors Museum

The museum houses the most exquisitely restored warriors, including the unique bronze chariot display and the famous General figure with intact color traces — a revelation after 2,000 years underground.
