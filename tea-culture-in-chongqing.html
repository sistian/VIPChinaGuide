Tea Culture in Chongqing

hongqing is home to some of China's most fascinating tea traditions - from historic tea houses to famous local varieties and time-honored brewing rituals.

Top Teas and Tea Houses in Chongqing

🍵

Jasmine Green Tea (茉莉花茶)

he most popular tea in Chongqing - jasmine-scented green tea served at every meal. The city's abundant street tea stalls serve it constantly.

Full name: Jasmine Tea (茉莉花茶)

🍶

Sweet Corn Tea (玉米茶)

Unique Chongqing staple - tea made from roasted corn kernels. Sold at street stalls throughout the city - warming, nutty, and refreshing.

Full name: Roasted Corn Tea (玉米茶)

🍵

Emei Shan Tea (峨眉山茶)

Green tea from nearby Emei Shan - one of Sichuan's Four Famous Mountains. Fresh, vegetal flavor available in Chongqing's tea houses.

Full name: Emei Shan Green Tea (峨眉山茶)

🏮

Ciqioukou Tea Market (磁器口茶馆)

Famous tea street market in the historic Ciqioukou ancient town - dozens of tea stalls serving local and provincial teas in traditional settings.

Full name: Ciqioukou Tea Street (磁器口茶街)

🍵

Chongqing Cold Tea (凉茶)

Traditional herbal teas unique to Chongqing's hot, humid climate. Brewed from medicinal herbs and roots - an essential part of local food culture to counter the heat.

Full name: Chongqing Herbal Tea (凉茶)

🍵

Sichuan Xinyang Mao Jian (信阳毛尖)

Henan green tea widely consumed in Chongqing - the light, refreshing flavor complements the spicy local cuisine.

Full name: Xinyang Mao Jian Green Tea (信阳毛尖)

History and Culture

The fascinating history behind Chongqing's tea culture

Ancient Sichuan

Tea Horse Road Origins

Sichuan is one of China's oldest tea regions - the ancient Tea Horse Road began here, trading tea with Tibet. Chongqing was a key port on the Yangtze for tea transport.

Qing Dynasty

City Tea Culture

Chongqing developed its own unique tea culture as a major Yangtze River port - teas from Yunnan and Sichuan converged here. The Ciqioukou area became famous for tea trading.

2026-2027

Tea Culture Thrives

Chongqing's tea houses are beloved by locals - the city has more tea houses per capita than any other Chinese city. New specialty tea venues have opened alongside the traditional stalls.
