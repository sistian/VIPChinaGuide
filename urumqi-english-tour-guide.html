📍 Urumqi City Overview

Area

4,216 km²

Urumqi City: urban and surrounding grasslands

Population

4.05 Million

Uyghur: ~25%, Han: ~65%, multi-ethnic city

Name Origin

Urumqi (乌鲁木齐) comes from the Mongolian word meaning 'Beautiful Pasture' — originally a grassland trading post on the Silk Road. It became the capital of Xinjiang in 1884 and is now the political, economic, and cultural center of China's far northwest, serving as the gateway to Central Asia.

Happiness Index

7.3/10 — A vibrant multicultural city with strong Uyghur, Kazakh, and Hui influences, famous for its food and music.

Metro & Transport

Airport: Urumqi Diwopu (URC) — 3 terminals (T1/T2/T3), 30 min to city center by Airport Bus (¥15) or Metro Line 1. Train Stations: Urumqi Railway Station (city center, near Erdaoqiao), Urumqi South (high-speed rail, 20 min to center). Getting Around: Metro Line 1 runs through the city; taxis are affordable. Note: Xinjiang has strict security checks at all transport hubs; carry ID at all times.

Universities

Xinjiang University (新疆大学), Xinjiang Medical University — major education and research institutions for Central Asian studies.

Natural Resources

Tian Shan Mountains (visible from the city), South Mountain Pasture (南山牧场), Hongshan Park, and the expansive Gobi Desert to the east. The city sits in a valley surrounded by snow-capped peaks.

🍜 Urumqi Food & Shopping Guide

🍽️ Must-Try Foods

  • Pilaf (手抓饭) — Xinjiang's signature: rice cooked with lamb, carrots, raisins, and onions, eaten by hand
  • Lamb Kebabs (烤羊肉串) — Chunky lamb skewers grilled over charcoal with cumin, chili, and salt, the ultimate Xinjiang street food
  • Lagman Noodles (拉条子) — Hand-pulled wheat noodles with spicy lamb and vegetable stir-fry, a Uyghur staple
  • Naan Bread (馕) — Round, dense bread baked in a tandoor oven, eaten with every meal and lasting for weeks
  • Yogurt (酸奶) — Thick, sour sheep's milk yogurt with honey and nuts, sold in clay bowls at bazaars
  • Hui BanMian (回族人拌面) — Hui ethnic cold noodles with sesame paste, chili oil, and shredded cucumber, a summer favorite

🛍️ Best Shopping Spots

  • Erdaoqiao Grand Bazaar (二道桥大巴扎) — The largest bazaar in Xinjiang, with Uyghur carpets, dried fruit, spices, and jade
  • International Grand Bazaar (国际大巴扎) — Modern tourist bazaar with Uyghur musical instruments, silk scarves, and nuts
  • Xinjiang Jade Market (新疆玉器) — Buy Hetian jade (white jade) from Hotan, the most prized jade in China
  • Dried Fruit Market (干果市场) — Raisins, apricots, walnuts, and pistachios from the Turpan oasis
  • Uyghur Musical Instrument Shop (乐器店) — Handcrafted dutar (two-string lute), rawap, and dap (frame drum) from Kashgar artisans

📸 Hot Photo Spots

  • Erdaoqiao Grand Bazaar (二道桥) — The iconic Islamic architecture with minarets and domes, bustling with Uyghur vendors and shoppers
  • Hongshan Park (红山公园) — The red mountain in the city center with a pagoda overlooking the urban sprawl
  • Tian Shan Mountains (天山) — Snow-capped peaks visible from the city on clear days, best viewed from the southern suburbs
  • Xinjiang Regional Museum (新疆博物馆) — The 3,800-year-old 'Beauty of Loulan' mummy and ancient Silk Road artifacts
  • Dabancheng Wind Farm (达坂城) — Rows of wind turbines against the Gobi Desert landscape, on the road to Turpan

🏛️ Intangible Heritage & Craftsmen

  • Uyghur Muqam (十二木卡姆) — Master Turdi Akhun preserves the 'Twelve Muqam,' a 1,500-year-old Uyghur musical tradition combining 360 songs, dances, and instrumental pieces, recognized by UNESCO as a masterpiece.
  • Xinjiang Carpet Weaving (新疆地毯) — Master Gulyamal at the Hotan Carpet Workshop hand-knots silk and wool carpets with Uyghur geometric patterns, a 2,000-year-old tradition along the Silk Road.
  • Uyghur Etles Silk (艾德莱斯绸) — Weaver Patigul in Kashgar creates the iconic 'Etles' silk with vibrant Ikat patterns, using tie-dye techniques brought from Central Asia 1,000 years ago.

🎭 Urumqi Concerts & Shows

Urumqi's performance culture reflects its position at the crossroads of the Silk Road — Uyghur classical music, Central Asian dance, and the famous Twelve Muqam are among the most extraordinary performances in China.

🎵

新疆木卡姆 (Xinjiang Twelve Muqam Performances)

The Uyghur Twelve Muqam is a UNESCO-listed art form — a complex fusion of music, poetry, song, and dance. Regular performances at the Xinjiang Theatre and cultural centers. A profound cultural experience.

💃

新疆民族歌舞 (Xinjiang Ethnic Song and Dance Performances)

Spectacular evening shows featuring traditional Uyghur, Kazakh, and Hui dance and music. Performed at major theaters and cultural venues — elaborate costumes, energetic dancing, and haunting melodies.

🎸

丝路之心 (Silk Road Heart)大型演出

The signature large-scale cultural performance in Urumqi — a theatrical show depicting the Silk Road history of Xinjiang with live music, dance, and visual effects. Usually performed at the Xinjiang Grand Theatre.

🎻

Urumqi International Music Festival

Held annually (usually July/August), featuring classical music performances by international and Chinese orchestras in the city's concert halls. Summer timing coincides with Xinjiang's best travel weather.

🏟️

Urumqi Sports Centre Concerts

Major pop and rock concerts are held at the Urumqi Sports Centre — international and Chinese pop artists occasionally perform in the city. Check local listings closer to your visit.

🎤

南疆大巴扎 (Southern Xinjiang Grand Bazaar) Cultural Shows

Evening cultural performances in the Grand Bazaar complex — traditional music, dance, and Uyghur cultural showcases. Open to all visitors, usually included in the bazaar admission.

🍵 Urumqi Tea Culture Tour

Urumqi is home to some of China's most fascinating tea traditions - from historic tea houses to famous local varieties and time-honored brewing rituals.

🍵

Kazakh Milk Tea (奶茶)

Kazakh traditional salted milk tea - black tea boiled with milk and salt. The daily drink of Kazakhstan culture in Xinjiang. Available at Kazakh restaurants and tea houses.

🍵

Uyghur Tea Culture (维吾尔茶)

Traditional Uyghur tea culture - black tea served with meals and social gatherings. Often sweetened and served in beautiful handleless glasses.

🫖

Nanhu Tea House District

Lakeside area with concentrations of traditional tea houses serving Kazakh and Uyghur teas. Popular afternoon gathering spot for locals.

🍵

Central Asian Tea Blends

Urumqi has unique tea traditions influenced by Central Asia - teas blended with herbs, rose petals, and dried fruits. Sold at the Grand Bazaar tea shops.

🍵

Xinjiang Black Tea (新疆红茶)

Black tea grown in Xinjiang's Ili Valley - unique local tea with Central Asian influences. Milder than typical Chinese black teas.

🍵

Grand Bazaar Tea Shops (大巴扎)

Famous tea merchants in the Grand Bazaar - selling Xinjiang teas, Central Asian blends, and teas from across China. Great for tea souvenirs.

Urumqi Xinjiang Museum & Heavenly Lake

Most Popular 1 Day

Discover the mummies of a 4,000-year-old Caucasian civilization in China's westernmost city, then ascend to a glacial lake surrounded by snow peaks and nomadic Kazakh yurts.

Xinjiang Regional Museum

Home to the world's most important collection of Tarim Basin mummies — 4,000-year-old bodies with blonde hair, blue eyes, and European features that prove ancient contact between East and West 2,000 years before the Silk Road. Your guide explains the mystery of the Beauty of Loulan, a mummified woman whose perfectly preserved facial features suggest she was of mixed Eurasian ancestry, and shows you 3,000-year-old textiles woven with patterns identical to those found in Celtic Europe.

Heavenly Lake (Tianchi)

A glacial lake at 1,907 meters in the Tianshan Mountains, surrounded by snow-capped peaks and Kazakh nomad settlements. Your guide arranges a boat ride on the 4.9-square-kilometer lake, explains the Kazakh tradition of eagle hunting where trained golden eagles catch foxes and wolves, and points out Bogda Peak at 5,445 meters — considered sacred by local Mongol and Kazakh peoples who make pilgrimage circuits around it.

Grand Bazaar (Erdaoqiao)

The largest bazaar in Northwestern China, covering 50,000 square meters with 3,000 stalls selling everything from Iranian saffron to Kyrgyz felt carpets. Your guide navigates you through the carpet section where Uyghur weavers create patterns passed down through 40 generations, explains the tradition of Uyghur knife-making where blades are folded 200 times, and arranges a tasting of Xinjiang's famous foods — lamb kebabs, hand-pulled noodles, and naan bread baked in clay tandoor ovens.
