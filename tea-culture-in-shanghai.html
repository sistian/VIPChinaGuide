Tea Culture in Shanghai

Shanghai is home to some of China's most fascinating tea traditions - from historic tea houses to famous local varieties and time-honored brewing rituals.

Top Teas and Tea Houses in Shanghai

🍵

Shanghai Morning Tea (早茶)

Shanghai's morning tea tradition - exquisite dim sum served with Longjing (Dragon Well) green tea or pu'er. A social ritual that locals practice daily at famous restaurants.

Full name: Morning Tea Culture (早茶)

🍃

Longjing Tea (龙井茶)

Hangzhou's famous Dragon Well green tea - available fresh in Shanghai's best tea houses. The flat, mellow leaves produce a distinctive chestnut aroma that Shanghai tea lovers cherish.

Full name: Dragon Well Tea (龙井茶)

🏮

Nanjing Road Tea Quarter

The area around Nanjing Road has a concentration of traditional tea houses where Shanghai's elite gather for afternoon tea ceremonies. High-end tea houses serve rare teas in elegant Qing Dynasty settings.

Full name: Nanjing Road Tea Quarter (南京路茶区)

🍵

Pu'er and Aged Tea Culture

Shanghai has developed a culture of aging pu'er and collecting aged white tea. Premium aged pu'er cakes from the 1990s are served in exclusive Shanghai tea houses.

Full name: Aged Pu'er Tea (老普洱)

🍶

Sweet Fermented Rice Tea (酒酿茶)

A unique Shanghai winter specialty - fermented rice pudding served in warm tea. Available at traditional dessert shops in the old town.

Full name: Sweet Fermented Rice Drink (酒酿茶)

🍵

Maojian Tea (毛尖茶)

Shanghai's nearby provinces produce famous green teas served in the city's traditional tea houses along with premium oolong varieties from Fujian.

Full name: Maojian Green Tea (毛尖茶)

History and Culture

The fascinating history behind Shanghai's tea culture

1840s

The Treaty Port Era

Shanghai's tea culture developed during the Treaty Port era when the city became China's gateway to the world. Western-style tea rooms mixed with traditional Chinese tea houses, creating Shanghai's unique fusion tea culture.

1920s-1930s

The Golden Age

The 1920s-30s was the golden age of Shanghai tea houses - they were centers of journalism, literature, and business. The famous Gongfu Cha ceremony was refined during this period.

2026-2027

Contemporary Tea Culture

Shanghai has become China's most cosmopolitan tea city - traditional tea houses coexist with modern tea bars. Premium aged pu'er culture has taken root with serious collectors. New Shanghai tea museums have opened.
