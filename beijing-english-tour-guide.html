📍 Beijing City Overview

Area

16,411 km²

Population

21.9 million

Name Origin

Beijing (北京) literally means "Northern Capital". It has served as the capital of multiple dynasties and is the political, cultural, and educational center of modern China.

Happiness Index

7.2/10 — Residents enjoy world-class cultural amenities, extensive parks, and modern infrastructure.

Metro & Transport

Airport: Beijing Capital (PEK) — 3 terminals, 30 min to city by Airport Express (¥25). Beijing Daxing (PKX) — 1 terminal, 46 min by Daxing Airport Express (¥35). Train Stations: Beijing South (high-speed rail hub, 20 min to Tiananmen), Beijing West (to Xian/Lhasa, 35 min), Beijing Railway Station (city center, walkable to Wangfujing), Beijing North (suburban trains to Great Wall).

Universities

Peking University, Tsinghua University, Renmin University

Natural Resources

Yan Mountains, Taihang Mountains, Miyun Reservoir, Fragrant Hills, Summer Palace gardens, and extensive urban greenways.

🗓️ Best Time to Visit Beijing

Plan your perfect Beijing trip with our seasonal guide — weather, events, and travel tips for every time of year.

🌸 Spring (Mar–May) Best Season

15–25°C

15–25°C, clear skies, low humidity. Ideal for the Forbidden City, Great Wall at Mutianyu, and Summer Palace. Beijing's most comfortable travel season.

25–35°C

25–35°C, hot with heavy rainfall in July–August. Crowds thinner, but rain can disrupt plans. Best for indoor attractions like the National Museum.

15–25°C

15–25°C, crisp air, golden ginkgo trees. Most recommended time to visit — comfortable temperatures and stunning autumn foliage.

❄️

-5–5°C

-5–5°C, dry and cold. Fewer tourists, but some attractions have reduced hours. Unique charm of snow-covered Forbidden City.

🍜 Beijing Food & Shopping Guide

🍽️ Must-Try Foods

  • Peking Duck (北京烤鸭) — Crispy skin carved tableside at Quanjude or Da Dong
  • Jianbing (煎饼) — Savory crepe with egg, scallion, and crispy wonton, best street breakfast
  • Zhajiangmian (炸酱面) — Noodles with fermented soybean paste, a Beijing staple
  • Mongolian Hotpot (涮羊肉) — Thin-sliced lamb in copper pot, legendary at Donglaishun
  • Luzhu Huoshao (卤煮火烧) — Offal stew with wheat bread, a working-class classic
  • Douzhi (豆汁) — Fermented mung bean drink, love-it-or-hate-it local breakfast

🛍️ Best Shopping Spots

  • Wangfujing (王府井) — Beijing's most famous pedestrian street, mix of luxury and snack stalls
  • Panjiayuan Antique Market (潘家园) — Weekend treasure hunting for jade, calligraphy, and curios
  • Qianmen Dajie (前门大街) — Restored Qing-era commercial street with time-honored brands
  • Silk Street (秀水街) — Bargain silk, pearls, and tailor-made suits
  • 798 Art District (798艺术区) — Galleries, design shops, and indie boutiques

📸 Hot Photo Spots

  • Jingshan Park pavilion (景山万春亭) — Best panoramic view of the Forbidden City at golden hour
  • The Corner Tower of the Forbidden City (角楼) — Iconic reflection shot at sunrise, especially the northwest corner
  • Houhai Lake at sunset (后海) — Paddle boats, willow trees, and traditional architecture silhouettes
  • The Great Wall at Mutianyu (慕田峪) — Dramatic mountain ridges with far fewer crowds than Badaling
  • The Olympic Park at night (鸟巢/水立方) — Futuristic LED-lit stadiums against the dark sky

🏛️ Intangible Heritage & Craftsmen

  • Beijing Cloisonné (景泰蓝) — Master Zhang Tonglu at the Beijing Enamel Factory continues this 600-year-old enamel-on-copper art. Watch artisans solder copper wires and fill them with mineral pigments before firing.
  • Peking Opera (京剧) — Mei Lanfang's legacy lives on at the Liyuan Theatre. The painted-face art (lianpu) and acrobatic martial arts require decades of training.
  • Beijing Dough Figurines (面人) — Master Tang Yun sculpts vivid figures from colored wheat dough using only a small bamboo knife and comb.

🌙 Beijing Nightlife & Late-Night Food

Beijing transforms at night — from imperial palace glow to neon-lit hutong bars. Here's where locals eat, drink, and play after dark.

🏮

Wangfujing Night Market

Beijing's most famous night market — 800 meters of sizzling street food stalls. Try candied hawthorn skewers, lamb skewers, and stinky tofu. Open until 10pm daily.

🍜

Donghuamen Night Market

One of Beijing's oldest night markets, near the Forbidden City. Specializes in unusual skewers — quail eggs, squid, lamb liver. A Beijing institution since the 1990s.

🥢

Ghost Street (Guijie)

400 meters of 24-hour restaurants in Dongzhimen — the ultimate Beijing late-night eating strip. Hot pot, BBQ, dumplings, and cold beer at any hour. A local favorite since the 1990s.

🍺

Sanlitun Bar Street

Beijing's nightlife epicenter — three streets of international bars, craft beer pubs, cocktail lounges, and clubs. The bar scene that put China on the global nightlife map.

🌙

Houhai Lakeside Bars

Traditional courtyard bars and live music venues around the scenic Houhai lakes — a more atmospheric alternative to Sanlitun with lakeside outdoor seating.

🎭

Chaoyang Theatre Evening Shows

The best place in Beijing to catch authentic Chinese variety shows, acrobatics, and traditional opera in the evenings — perfect after dinner.

🎭 Beijing Concerts and Shows

Beijing hosts the finest performing arts in China — from the National Centre for the Performing Arts to intimate hutong jazz bars. Here's where to experience world-class performances.

🎭

Peking Opera (京剧) Performances

Beijing is the home of Peking Opera — catch authentic performances at the Lao She Tea House (老舍茶馆) or the National Centre for the Performing Arts (NCPA). Shows feature elaborate costumes, acrobatics, and the distinctive singing style. English program notes sometimes available.

🎻

National Centre for the Performing Arts (NCPA) Concerts

Beijing's premier classical music venue — the NCPA hosts the Beijing Philharmonic, world-class ballet, opera, and international guest orchestras. Book tickets well ahead for major performances.

🎸

Beijing International Music Festival

Held annually in September/October — one of Asia's most prestigious classical music festivals, featuring internationally renowned conductors and soloists at venues across the city.

🎤

Workers' Stadium (工人体育场) Concerts

Beijing's largest concert venue — major Chinese and international pop and rock artists perform here. The stadium also hosts large-scale sporting events and festivals.

🍵

Lao She Tea House Traditional Performances

Beijing's most famous traditional tea house — nightly Peking Opera excerpts, traditional instrumental music, and calligraphy performances in an authentic hutong setting. Tea service included with ticket.

🎷

402 Jazz Club and Houhai Live Music Bars

Beijing's legendary jazz scene — the 402 Jazz Club and venues around Houhai Lakeside have live jazz, blues, and world music every night. International and Chinese jazz musicians perform nightly.

🍵 Beijing Tea Culture Tour

Beijing is home to some of China's most fascinating tea traditions - from historic tea houses to famous local varieties and time-honored brewing rituals.

🍵

Beijing Jasmine Tea (茉莉花茶)

Beijing's signature tea - jasmine flowers scented over green tea leaves. Famed tea houses like Lao She Tea House serve it with traditional snacks. The city's dry climate makes jasmine tea particularly popular.

🏺

Lao She Tea House (老舍茶馆)

Beijing's most iconic tea house - traditional performers, authentic Peking opera excerpts, and authentic jasmine tea service. A cultural experience not to be missed.

🍶

Kaiyuan Market Tea Shopping

Famous tea market near Qianmen where visitors can taste and purchase authentic jasmine tea, pu'er, and other famous Chinese teas at reasonable prices.

🍵

Chrysanthemum Tea (菊花茶)

Classic Beijing winter warmer - dried chrysanthemum flowers brewed hot with rock sugar. Sold at street vendors and traditional tea houses throughout the city.

🍵

Pu'er Tea Culture in Beijing

Beijing has a long tradition of aging and collecting pu'er tea from Yunnan. Many traditional tea houses have pu'er cakes aged for decades.

🏯

Summer Palace Tea Houses

Historic tea houses inside the Summer Palace grounds serve imperial-style tea service in beautiful lakeside settings - a serene tea experience.

⚽ Beijing Major Sports Events

Beijing hosts major national and international sporting events throughout the year - from world-class marathons to professional tournaments.

🏃

Beijing International Marathon (北京马拉松)

Held annually in autumn - one of China's most prestigious marathons. The 42km course passes through Tiananmen Square, the Forbidden City, and the Olympic Green. Open to international runners. 2026 edition marks the 40th anniversary.

China Open Tennis Championships (中国网球公开赛)

Major international tennis tournament held at the National Tennis Center in Chaoyang District. Combined men's and women's event in September-October. Top ATP and WTA players compete for ranking points.

Beijing National Alpine Ski Centre Events

Host venue of the 2022 Winter Olympics - now open for public skiing and international winter sports events. Hosts World Cup ski events each winter season.

CBA Basketball League - Beijing Ducks Home Games

Chinese Basketball Association regular season and playoff games at the Workers' Gymnasium. International star players join each season.

⚽

Beijing Guoan FC - Chinese Super League

Home matches at the Workers' Stadium (工人体育场). Chinese Super League season runs March-November. Tickets available through official channels or at the stadium on match day.

Beijing Winter Sports Championships

Annual winter sports events at Olympic venues including speed skating, figure skating, and ice hockey competitions. Excellent spectator experience at former Olympic venues.

Classic Beijing Imperial Tour

Most Popular 1 Day

Walk through 600 years of imperial history at the world's largest palace complex, then hike the Great Wall at its most dramatic mountain setting.

Forbidden City (Palace Museum)

The world's largest ancient wooden palace complex, spanning 720,000 square meters with 9,999 rooms. Built between 1406-1420, it served as the imperial palace for 24 emperors across Ming and Qing dynasties. Walk through the Meridian Gate, Hall of Supreme Harmony with its dragon throne, and the Imperial Garden with 400-year-old cypress trees. Your guide explains Feng Shui principles, the symbolism of yellow glazed tiles reserved exclusively for imperial buildings, and the secret lives of emperors who ruled from these halls.

Tiananmen Square

The world's largest city square at 440,000 square meters, capable of holding one million people. Your guide walks you through its layered history — from the Ming Dynasty's Gate of Heavenly Peace built in 1420, to the 1949 founding ceremony of the People's Republic where Mao Zedong proclaimed 'The Chinese people have stood up,' to its role as the symbolic heart of modern China.

Mutianyu Great Wall

One of the most scenic and best-preserved sections of the Great Wall, stretching 2,250 meters along mountain ridges covered in forest. Built in 1368 during the Ming Dynasty, it features 23 watchtowers connected by steep stone staircases. Your guide explains the wall's military beacon fire communication system — smoke by day and fire by night could transmit news of invasion across 5,000 kilometers in just hours — and the legendary stories of the million workers who built it over 2,000 years.

Beijing Culture & Hutong Discovery

Cultural 1 Day

Explore the living heart of old Beijing through narrow alleyways, courtyard homes, and centuries-old temples where locals still practice traditional customs unchanged for generations.

Hutong Alleys (Nanluoguxiang)

A 786-year-old hutong dating to the Yuan Dynasty, stretching 786 meters with 16 sub-lanes. Your guide takes you through grey-brick courtyard houses (siheyuan), explains the social hierarchy encoded in gate decoration — officials had taller thresholds than merchants — visits local families whose families have lived here for six generations, and shows hidden teahouses where Beijing opera singers still practice daily.

Temple of Heaven

A masterpiece of Ming architecture built in 1420 where emperors performed annual ceremonies praying for good harvests. The circular Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests stands on a three-tiered marble terrace built without a single nail, its blue roof symbolizing heaven. Your guide demonstrates the Echo Wall where whispers travel clearly along a 65-meter curve, and explains the cosmic symbolism of the circular temple within a square outer wall representing 'heaven is round, earth is square.'

Lama Temple (Yonghegong)

The largest Tibetan Buddhist temple outside Tibet, converted from an imperial palace in 1744. It houses an 18-meter tall sandalwood Buddha statue carved from a single Tibetan sandalwood tree — certified by Guinness World Records. Your guide explains Tibetan Buddhist rituals, the clockwise rotation of prayer wheels which sends prayers to heaven with each turn, and how this temple uniquely bridges Han Chinese and Tibetan Buddhist cultures under one roof.

Modern Beijing & Olympic Legacy

Modern Half Day

Witness China's contemporary face through cutting-edge architecture, Olympic venues, and vibrant art districts that blend industrial heritage with creative energy.

Bird's Nest (National Stadium)

The iconic 91,000-seat stadium designed by architects Herzog and de Meuron for the 2008 Olympics. Its steel lattice structure weighs 42,000 tons and required 36 kilometers of concealed welding with no two beams identical. Your guide explains the architectural symbolism of a 'nest' nurturing future generations, and how Chinese engineers solved the challenge of building an earthquake-resistant structure on Beijing's fault line.

798 Art District

A sprawling 600,000-square-meter former electronics factory complex transformed into China's most important contemporary art zone. Soviet-built Bauhaus factories now house over 400 galleries. Your guide navigates you through cutting-edge installations, street art by anonymous political artists, and explains how this district became the epicenter of China's underground art movement after artists first squatted here in 1995.

CCTV Headquarters

The 234-meter headquarters nicknamed 'Big Pants' — a gravity-defying loop structure where two towers lean at 6 degrees and connect 162 meters in the air via a cantilevered bridge. Your guide explains architect Rem Koolhaas's vision of 'anti-icon' architecture and the structural engineering breakthroughs that made this impossible-looking building withstand Beijing's earthquakes and typhoon winds.
